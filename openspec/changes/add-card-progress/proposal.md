## Why

`add-trips` built the shelf and stopped there. A trip gathers members, invite links, and frozen card snapshots under one title, and every one of those cards is inert: a member can look at a card assigned to them, and that is the entire interaction. The feature that people came for — actually spotting a windmill, a red convertible, or someone saying "let's take this offline," and marking it — does not exist. The add-trips design said so plainly, listing "live multiplayer gameplay, marking state, or winner detection" as the next change and deliberately leaving `tripCardId` as "the stable progress anchor" with `assignedMemberId` as "the forward-compatible anchor for future per-member progress."

This change collects on that seam. A member marks squares on a card they are entitled to play; every other member of the trip sees those marks appear without reloading the page; and the marked card exports as an image so it can be posted to a group chat that has nothing to do with this application. Marking is the whole of play mode's ground floor — win conditions and notifications are separate changes stacked on top of it, and both need this one's data model and its rendering to exist first.

Two constraints shape the design more than anything else. The first is that **the card renderer is frozen**: `CardGrid.tsx` and the `.bingo-*` rules in `App.css` render user data and feed four consumers at once — the screen, the print stylesheet, the PNG exporter, and the saved-card thumbnail. A translucent X that shows up on screen but not in the exported image would be a broken feature, so the mark has to live inside the frozen renderer, which means extending it on purpose, with the guard test and the design document updated in the same breath rather than after the fact. The second is that **the server is the authority on who may mark and when**. Whether a card is yours to play and whether the trip is currently running are both decidable from data the backend already holds, so neither may be asserted by the client; the frontend computes the same answers only to grey out a control and explain itself.

## What Changes

- **A member can mark and unmark squares on a trip card.** Each square on a trip card carries a marked/unmarked state, toggled one square at a time. Marking is not a bulk operation and there is no "clear card" — an accidental mark is undone by tapping the same square again.
- **Who may mark follows the assignment rule the trips spec already established.** In a **competitive** trip, only the member the card is assigned to may mark it; a card sitting in the unassigned pool is playable by nobody, including the administrator. In a **cooperative** trip, where the existing spec says "every member plays every card," any member may mark any card and all of them share one progress state per card. Administrator is not a play permission: an admin has no special right to mark a card assigned to someone else.
- **Everyone sees everyone's progress.** Progress is readable by every member of the trip regardless of who may modify it, extending the existing "all members can see all cards; assignment only decides who plays" rule to cover marks. The trip page refreshes progress on its own while it is open, so a member watching a trip-mate play sees squares fill in without touching anything.
- **Progress is locked outside the trip's date range.** When a trip carries a start date, an end date, or both, marks may only be modified inside that window. The window is evaluated generously against UTC so no traveller is shut out early or late by their own offset, it is enforced by the server, and the interface disables marking and says why rather than failing silently. A trip with no dates is always open.
- **Marks render as a translucent X inside the card itself.** The X is drawn over the square, not in place of it, so the underlying text stays readable — that is the point of the translucency, and it is what makes a marked card worth sharing. Because the mark lives inside the frozen renderer, it appears identically on screen, in print, and in the exported image.
- **A card being played can be exported as an image with its progress.** The existing PNG export gains a second caller: a trip card exports through the same code path, producing an image that carries the marks, so a member can post their progress somewhere this application has never heard of.
- **The frozen-renderer guard is extended deliberately.** The renderer's class allowlist grows to cover the marking layer, the design document records that the layer exists and what it may not do, and the export regression checklist is run because the renderer changed. The guard is not weakened: no application design tokens and no `oklch()` enter the card, and no new element type is introduced.
- **Unchanged:** the saved-card shape and both contract tests; the signed-out editor, which still makes zero API requests and gains no play affordance; the card entry pool, generation, and randomization; how trip cards are added, assigned, or removed; the trip roles; and the appearance of an unmarked card, which is byte-for-byte what it is today.

## Capabilities

### New Capabilities
- `card-progress`: Per-square marked state on a trip card — who may modify it, when the trip's dates allow it, how it is shown to every member of the trip, and how it reaches an exported image.

### Modified Capabilities
- `trips`: Card assignment gains a second consequence — it now decides who may mark, not only who nominally plays — and the all-members-see-all-cards rule is extended to cover each card's progress.
- `card-print-export`: The export surface grows a second subject. A card being played exports as an image carrying its marks, through the same in-browser, no-network path as the editor's card.
- `ui-development-workflow`: The card renderer's protective allowlist covers the marking layer, and the visual-review step for a renderer change is stated as "confirmed correct" rather than "confirmed unaffected", because this change intentionally changes it.

## Impact

- **Backend** (`backend/src/`): a third authorization sibling `requireTripCardPlayer` in `auth.ts` alongside `requireCardRole`/`requireTripRole`; a new pure `lib/playWindow.ts`; mark/unmark and progress-read handlers in `routes/trips.ts`; three route keys in `router.ts`. Marks are a new attribute on the existing `TRIP#`/`TRIPCARD#` item — no new table, no new GSI, no new key prefix, no migration.
- **Frontend** (`frontend/src/`): two optional props on the frozen `components/CardGrid.tsx` and three new `.bingo-*` rules in `App.css`, with `components/cardGrid.guard.test.ts` and `DESIGN.md` updated in the same change; a `hooks/useTripProgress.ts` polling hook; a `lib/playWindow.ts` mirror; PNG export lifted out of `components/CardView.tsx` into a reusable `lib/cardPngExport.ts`; play and export controls on `pages/TripDetailPage.tsx`.
- **Infra** (`infra/`): three API Gateway HTTP API route keys, all authenticated. No new compute, storage, identity, or DNS resources.
- **CI/CD** (`.github/workflows/`): unchanged; the existing lint/test/build gates cover this change.
- **Contract tests**: the saved-card shape is untouched, so `cardPayload.contract.test.ts` and `savedCard.contract.test.ts` are unchanged. `TripCardSnapshot` is unchanged — progress is a sibling attribute on the item, not a field inside the snapshot.
- **No dependencies added** in either package. `html-to-image` is already present and is reused.
- **Out of scope**: win conditions and winner detection (`add-win-conditions`); notifications of any kind (`add-play-notifications`, `add-email-notifications`); real-time push transport — progress refresh is polling, and a WebSocket API remains out of the infrastructure; per-member private progress in cooperative trips; undo history or an audit trail of who marked what; and progress on saved cards outside a trip, which stay account-free documents.
